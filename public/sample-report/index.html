Fictional example for illustration only. This is not a client report, a technical test result or advice about your business. Your assessment, priorities and timing would depend on your evidence and agreed scope.

The example business

Imagine a small South African wholesale distributor. Your team relies on email for orders, a cloud accounting system for invoicing and a shared drive for customer and supplier records. An external IT provider supports your systems.

For this example, assume payment changes are confirmed by email alone, backup recovery has not been demonstrated, and some former staff accounts remain active.

Executive summary

Three risks to focus on.

01

Payment instructions could be misused

If someone impersonates a supplier or takes over an email account, your team could pay money into the wrong account.

What we would recommend

Confirm bank detail changes through a known phone number. Require a second approval for changes and review multifactor authentication on finance accounts with your IT provider.

Suggested owner: Finance lead, supported by your IT provider.

02

Your team may struggle to recover after an outage

Without evidence of a successful restore, you may not know how quickly orders and invoices could resume if key data became unavailable.

What we would recommend

Agree which systems you need first and how much interruption your business can tolerate. Ask your IT provider to demonstrate a safe restore and record the recovery steps.

Suggested owner: Operations lead, supported by your IT provider.

03

Old accounts could expose your business data

Accounts that stay active after people leave create unnecessary access to your customer records, files and systems.

What we would recommend

Review active accounts and permissions with system owners. Confirm which access is still needed and introduce a simple joiner and leaver checklist.

Suggested owner: Office manager, supported by system owners.

Illustrative priorities, not scored findings. Payment controls are a starting point in this scenario; recovery planning and access review can proceed alongside them. We would confirm likelihood, business impact and existing controls with you before agreeing your risk register.

Illustrative 90-day roadmap

Small steps. Clear ownership.

Days 1 to 30

Agree and act

Confirm payment checks, review finance account protections and identify your critical systems. Agree owners for each action.

Days 31 to 60

Build confidence

Coordinate a safe restore exercise with your IT provider. Review accounts and document your joiner and leaver checklist.

Days 61 to 90

Review together

Review evidence of completed actions, update your risk register and agree the next priorities with management.

Example timing only. Technical changes and testing require separate scope, authorisation and delivery arrangements. Your roadmap would reflect your resources and business dependencies.
