FIELDNOTES / 03 — THE GUIDED JOURNEY

A conversation with
a way forward.

Taute Group facilitates the session. Your team brings the context. Structured prompts help us explore possibilities, then turn discussion into practical decisions. We start from how your work actually happens, then widen the conversation to people, everyday operations and business consequences. The sequence below is adapted to the scope we agree together.

  1. 01

    Set the context

    Choose an important piece of work. Agree what matters, who should be involved and where the discussion begins and ends.

  2. 02

    Map important work

    Sketch the people, information, systems and suppliers involved. Mark handovers and assumptions that need another voice in the room.

  3. 03

    Explore what-ifs

    Introduce scenario cards: unavailable information, mistaken access or a disrupted supplier. Ask what could cause trouble and how it might unfold.

  4. 04

    Look at safeguards

    Explore existing checks, recovery arrangements and workarounds. Separate what the team knows from what someone still needs to verify.

  5. 05

    Weigh the consequences

    Trace the effects on customers, colleagues, time and trust. Discuss what is tolerable, what is uncertain and the trade-offs of a response.

  6. 06

    Choose actions

    Choose a manageable set of actions or checks. Name an owner, agree what completion means and decide when to revisit each one.

  7. 07

    Recap and follow up

    Bring the map, discussed scenarios, decisions and unanswered questions into a recap. Agree what needs follow-up or separately scoped specialist help.

FIELDNOTES / 05 — OUR APPROACH

A clear scope.
A shared conversation.

Taute Group is a small consultancy offering guided cybersecurity sessions, not a platform to work through alone. Your facilitator guides the questions; your team brings the knowledge of your business, including what is still uncertain.

Before a session, we agree the focus, who should take part and what the recap should cover. During the session, structured prompts make room for different perspectives. The aim is a shared picture and manageable next steps—not a promise that every risk is covered.

Session format, scope and any follow-up are agreed together before work begins.

What could we leave with?

A draft map of important work and dependencies, discussed scenarios, existing safeguards and open questions, plus chosen actions with owners and a recap. Exact outputs would be agreed before a session.

Do we need to be security specialists?

No. The starting point is how your team works. Technical colleagues or your IT provider can contribute where useful; unfamiliar terms can be unpacked together.

What is this not?

This is guided discussion, not an automated scanner, self-service security game, penetration test, audit, certification, compliance assurance or numerical security score. It does not guarantee safety or replace separately scoped technical work.
